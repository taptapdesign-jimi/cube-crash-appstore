# Stack to Six visual reference guide

Authored reference library, 2026-10-17. These descriptions were derived from viewed PNG artwork, not filenames alone. Reference images are authoritative; prose is an aid. Use original-resolution copies for generation. This guide describes appearance, not gameplay rules.

## Shared render language

Soft sculpted 3D toy/diorama rendering. Rounded bevels, inflated volumes, warm cream highlights and peach/brown contact shading. Broad soft light, gentle ambient occlusion in joints and layered objects. Matte or satin surfaces, with stronger gloss only on authored liquids, bubbles and polished details. Cream, honey yellow, coral orange and sage/lime greens recur; turquoise and teal create accents. Texture is simplified but tangible: subtle wood grain, sculpted foliage and soft fabric. Preserve each reference’s own palette rather than applying one universal orange tint. No heavy black contour, photoreal skin, gritty realism or sharp low-poly facets. Match perspective and light direction to the selected reference rather than assuming a fixed camera for all assets.

## Recurring human character — identity

Slim adult male caricature with oversized expressive head, swept light grey hair, glasses, prominent nose/ears, friendly smile and short facial hair. Cream short-sleeved shirt, narrow teal trousers and pale sneakers recur. Long thin bent limbs and large gestures give a playful silhouette. Keep face shape, glasses, hair, beard, outfit and body ratio consistent across new poses; inspect multiple poses before drawing. Accessories and poses vary by activity; they do not define a new person.

## Human with dog — paired identity

Use these as the primary pair references. The dog has a tan/golden body, darker brown muzzle/face areas and ear details, large upright ears, pale chest/muzzle accents, dark nose and a lively friendly expression. Preserve its specific silhouette and face markings; do not substitute an arbitrary dog breed. The human and dog share the same warm softly shaded sculpted finish. Match their relative scale from the chosen full-body pair. A new park scene should read both pair references plus an environment reference; the scene may change while their identities stay stable.

## Home slider — hero compositions

Compact floating arrangements of many rounded objects with a readable central subject and a clean outer silhouette. Journey mixes adventure/world props, Arcade emphasises dice and playful game tools, Settings uses workshop tools. Keep the visual mass grouped, with small orbiting props supporting rather than obscuring the hero. These are composition references; use close-up Shop art for individual prop detail.

## Ordinary dice — bodies and markings

The ordinary die is a thick rounded square of pale cream wood, subtle vertical/curving grain, a broad softly bevelled top and warmer lower edge. Preserve the authored wood rather than converting it into shiny plastic. Body PNGs and number atlases are separate references: inspect the number atlas before designing faces; never invent pip/number conventions from a blank body.

## Wild core art

Plain Wild Star is a plump rounded golden five-point star with warm edge shading; Magnet is a coral/red rounded horseshoe with pale ends and subtle grain; Juice is a coral cup with cream lid, bent striped straw and raised pale star. These are distinct object/material identities sharing one render language. New art must not silently introduce gameplay rules.

## Wild / Special — themed object families

Use a complete object PNG to establish silhouette, then inspect adjacent poses/parts for construction. Forest uses sculpted mushroom, honey, barrel and Bee/foliage; Beach includes fish, beach ball and drinks; Area 55 includes spaceship, LaserGun, robot and Kanta. These associations guide visual art, not current reward eligibility. Cubero is a cream cube character with a coral headband and expressive face; retain its authored facial features rather than giving it the human character’s face. Metal bands, honey and glass have their own highlights. Do not use a sprite sheet, detached shard or animation fragment as the only identity reference.

## Journey worlds — Forest

Floating rounded grassy diorama with a warm chunky earth/stone base, stylized tiered conifers, wooden fence and sign, tent and campfire. Soft yellow-green vegetation, cream tent and orange wood; generous rounded masses and simplified details. View the complete world for layout and close-up props for material. Keep the island’s underside and camera elevation consistent when extending it.

## Journey worlds — Beach

Rounded sandy island meeting a thick turquoise water volume, with palm trees, umbrella, beach props and playful characters. Cream/golden sand and cyan/teal water contrast with coral/yellow accessories. Soft white water-edge accents and glossy water stay stylized. Preserve the authored island volume and horizon-free diorama presentation.

## Journey worlds — Area 55 / Robo

Warm sandy sci-fi diorama with a friendly rounded saucer, cyan illuminated windows/beams, rocky desert props and small robot details. The environment remains cream/sand with teal accents; do not replace it with a generic dark neon space scene. Same soft miniature scale and rounded floating base as the other worlds.

## Effects — supporting visual vocabulary

Soft translucent soap bubbles use pale cream/pink interiors and bright edge reflections. Crumble debris has warm textured irregular chunks and readable volume. Smoke, sparks, glows and object-specific fragments should match their own reference family and remain visually subordinate to the subject. PNGs describe shape/color only; animation timing, particles, layering and cleanup come from the existing runtime owners and contracts. Never infer motion from a single still or bake a checkerboard into transparency.
