## 2. Overall visual language

A warm, sunny, softly colored, fully volumetric stylized 3D illustration with rounded forms and an affectionate retro storybook/game-adventure feeling. The finish
is clean and luminous: pale cream highlights, peach mids, yellow-gold accents,
muted olive/sage greens and a small clean aqua/cyan accent. All major forms have
soft continuous curvature, plump corners, generous bevels and calm broad shading.
The image is crisp enough to read at small Home-icon size without harsh texture.
Retro means gentle familiar shapes and warm color relationships, not film grain,
paper distress, brown ageing, scratches, a sepia overlay or faded unreadable art.
Do not translate “soft” into blur, white haze or lost contours.

## 3. Light, exposure, reflections and shadows

The visible image has broad cream-lit upward/front-facing surfaces and gentle
warm shading beneath overlapping forms. Treat the precise light angle as an
approximation from the visible forms, not an exact measured rig. Use a large,
soft source and ample fill. Midtones remain bright; skin, shirt, dog chest,
clouds and card edges remain distinguishable. Shadows gather softly under the
chin, glasses, arms, paws, chest lid, coins and leaves; their edges are broad and
their darkness restrained. Contact still anchors objects. No dirty mottled
shadow texture, deep brown ambient-occlusion trenches or black outline around
every object. The mouth/eyes/nose can have their natural local dark values.

Warm reflected light is subtle and follows the curved object. The gold/peach
props can feel gently radiant without throwing an orange tint over skin, shirt,
blue jeans or aqua elements. Bright edges are rounded highlight bands, not
crispy white outlines. Stronger specular shine belongs to honey/liquid, coins
and small polished details; it is not a wet plastic coat on skin or every prop.
No exterior yellow/white glow halo in transparent pixels.

## No outline or ink contour — explicit user correction

Use fully volumetric stylized 3D rendering with NO drawn outlines: no stroke,
line-art, ink contour, cel/toon outline, comic edging, silhouette tracing or
uniform edge bands. This forbids thin/subtle beige, peach, brown, gray, colored
and white outlines as well as thick black ones. Do not trace the outside of
skin, hands, clothing, shoes, dog, props, wood planks, islands, stones or leaves.
“Stylized” describes proportions and shapes, never a line-drawn cartoon finish.

Separate forms through actual 3D volume, broad light gradients, material changes,
soft depth/occlusion and local contact shadows. Natural edges change with light,
curvature and overlap; they do not have a constant colored line running around
them. A real seam/recess may have a soft local shadow but must not become a drawn
border. Retain physical eyeglass frames, intended printed text/symbols/dice marks
and natural mouth/eye features; these are objects/design details, not permission
to outline every shape. Do not flatten the master into cel shading or a 2D/3D
hybrid. Review silhouette edges and internal material boundaries for unwanted
contour strokes before delivery. The approved image remains the visual authority.

## 4. Surface and texture boundary — critical

Skin is smooth and even, with broad peach-to-cream shading and very quiet tonal
variation. No pores, elderly creases, cracked clay, leathery patches, cellular
noise, polygon facets, quilting, wool, felt, flocking or cloth grain on skin.
Hands/forearms/nose/ears must share the same clean skin finish. Do not repeat
the rejected dirty, angular patchwork or fabric-like surface under softer light.

The master at presentation size does contain quiet material-specific cues: a
short soft gray hair/beard mass, subtle blue denim, faint warm wood structure
and soft dog-fur contours. Keep those subordinate to the large clean form.
Do not magnify or invent them as dense microdetail on a higher-resolution image.
Fine hair belongs to hair; wood structure belongs to wood. Do not spread one
procedural texture over skin, clothes, trees, cards and clouds. Use material
changes and smooth lighting to separate objects, rather than added grit.
